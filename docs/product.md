# Product brief

## Owner-stated direction

The game's official name is **Medieval Economy** (confirmed by the owner on 2026-09-08).

A slow-paced medieval browser game in which each player manages a village. Visual and thematic references include Age of Empires and Forge of Empires; timed-action interaction was compared to The Crims. These are inspirations, not instructions to reproduce assets or exact mechanics.

Gather resources, construct and improve a village, develop expertise, produce goods, and trade with other players. Activities take real time and the world persists while players are offline.

All professions may be developed, while deep specialization unlocks new goods and production capabilities. Examples include mining, farming, cooking, refining, and crafting. Advanced production depends on materials from early through late progression. A level-100 platinum armor example illustrated this principle; level counts, names, and recipes are not finalized.

Advanced villages can gather basic resources. Their incentive to buy is the real time and productive capacity sacrificed by gathering everything themselves. Do not prohibit low-level gathering or impose anti-expansion limits to solve balance. Keep useful opportunities at every progression level.

One village per account. Multiple accounts are explicitly allowed; twenty fully active villages require twenty subscriptions. Profitable multi-village ownership is legitimate play. Spending money to buy player-produced goods and accelerate progress is acceptable. Avoid developer sales of exclusive overpowered goods.

Long-term: real-money player trading, an intended 5% buyer surcharge, optional premium shops that add visibility and automatic offline selling, and eventual sales of valuables or accounts/villages. Direct trading remains possible without a premium shop. One Crown to open a shop was an example; price and duration are undecided.

## Launch direction under consideration

Free temporary testing during development, followed by closing the test world and starting a fresh paid world. Clearly disclose resets. Recommendation: use nonredeemable test money only during testing. Exact launch commitments and subscription price remain open.

## Recommended alpha defaults, not final owner decisions

Limited shared workforce creates opportunity cost. Automatic output delivery, small connected production chains, test-currency marketplace, original static placeholder art. One regional world map can follow the basic economy. No combat or real payment integration in the initial slice.

## Open decisions

Exact building placement and worker rules; progression formulas; final resource catalog; map, transport and exploration rules; warfare; real-money settlement/provider/jurisdictions; crypto versus regular payments; Crown behavior; subscription pricing and expiration; transfer scope; recurring item consumption and durability.

Crypto was discussed and a regular game was recommended, but the owner has not explicitly finalized the choice. Do not launch a token or implement blockchain without a later decision.

## Success questions

Can a new village find demand for its output? Does buying basic inputs save worthwhile production time? Does mastery create distinct useful capabilities? Do finite workers create interesting decisions? Is the normal cadence enjoyable? Can players trust every inventory and market transaction?
